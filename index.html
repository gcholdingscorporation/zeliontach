Harmonics and spool-up

A rotor radiates a tone at rpm × blades ÷ 60 — 33–200 Hz for two blades at 1 000–6 000 rpm. Microphones roll off steeply down there, so the fundamental can be 25 dB below its own harmonics and a naive peak pick reports 2× or 4× the real speed. The detector searches from 300 rpm upward regardless of your expected range, then walks back down to the true fundamental. Readings under your minimum are shown and marked, not forced into range.

Getting a clean reading

Stand 2–5 m off, downwind, with the mic unobstructed. Watch the level meter — clipping invents harmonics and the reading drifts high. The faint ticks along the spectrum baseline mark where harmonics of the current lock should fall: if they sit on real peaks, the lock is right.

Not an airworthiness instrument

This is a tuning aid for RC models. It is uncertified, it can be fooled by a strong harmonic, and it must not be used to decide whether any rotor system is safe to fly. Verify against an optical tach before you trust it for anything that matters.
